What the cells do

What stem cells may do as you age

With age, the body tends to run a quiet, constant inflammation that researchers call “inflammaging”, and its own repair systems slow down. Mesenchymal stem cells are studied because they send signals that act on both.

01

They may turn down low-grade inflammation

The cells release signals that can lower inflammatory messengers such as TNF-alpha. In trials in frail older adults, these markers came down after the infusion. Less background inflammation may mean better energy and recovery.

02

They may help rebalance the immune system

An aging immune system tends to stay switched on, with more activated cells and less control. In human studies, mesenchymal stem cells reduced markers of immune activation. The hope is an immune system that reacts when it should and rests when it can.

03

They support muscles, vessels and repair

The cells release growth factors, proteins that help other cells survive and repair. In trials in older adults, this was followed in some cases by better walking distance, grip strength and physical performance.

04

The data tells you where you started

The cancer markers, MRI and DEXA scan give you a baseline of your organs, bone density and body composition. That way the functional-medicine doctor can follow what changes over time.

What the evidence says

What the research shows so far

Research on stem cells and aging has focused on frail older adults, people whose strength and energy had already declined. Here is what those randomized trials found.

Lab & animal studiesFirst human studiesSeveral clinical trialsStandard of care

Older adults with frailty who received umbilical cord stem cells through a vein reported better physical quality of life and did better on walking and grip tests than those given placebo, with lower inflammatory markers at six months.

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 1/2 trial of umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells in China. Side effects were no more frequent than with placebo.

Safety and efficacy of umbilical cord tissue-derived mesenchymal stem cells in the treatment of patients with aging frailty: a phase I/II randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. · Stem Cell Research & Therapy, 2024 ↗

In 147 frail adults aged 60 to 85, two umbilical cord stem cell infusions were followed by better physical performance scores at nine months than supplements alone, with no serious side effects.

Phase 2 randomized trial in Vietnam; both groups took oral supplements.

Safety and efficacy of allogeneic umbilical cord-derived mesenchymal stem cell infusion for frailty: a phase 2, single-centre, randomised, open-label controlled trial. · EBioMedicine, 2026 ↗

In the largest blinded trial so far, frail older adults who received mesenchymal stem cells walked farther in six minutes than those given placebo, and the effect grew with the dose.

Randomized, placebo-controlled phase 2b trial, 148 ambulatory older adults with frailty in the United States; donor bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cells given intravenously. The difference was clearest at nine months.

Randomized phase 2b dose-escalation trial of stem cell therapy with laromestrocel for aging frailty. · Cell Stem Cell, 2026 ↗

A review of the randomized trials concluded that intravenous mesenchymal stem cells are generally well tolerated in older adults with frailty and show signals of better mobility, quality of life and inflammation.

Systematic review of three randomized controlled trials, about 208 participants in total.

Safety and Efficacy of Mesenchymal Stem Cell Therapy in Aging Frailty: A Systematic Review. · International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2026 ↗

In short: The trials are randomized, consistent on safety, and show encouraging signals in mobility, quality of life and inflammation in older adults. That is why the protocol measures first: your physician reads your numbers with you, and your plan is built on your own data.
