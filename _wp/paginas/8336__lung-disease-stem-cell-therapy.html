What the cells do

What stem cells may do in the lungs

When mesenchymal stem cells are given through a vein, many of them pass through the lungs first. That is one reason researchers study them for lung disease: they may act on the inflammation and scarring right where it happens.

01

They calm chronic inflammation

In COPD and fibrosis, the lungs stay inflamed for years, and that inflammation keeps damaging the tissue. The cells release signals that are studied for turning down this inflammation, which could mean slower damage over time.

02

They may slow scarring signals

Fibrosis happens when the lung keeps laying down scar tissue, a stiff material that makes breathing harder. In laboratory studies, mesenchymal stem cells reduce the signals that drive that scarring. In people, the aim is to slow it.

03

They support the air sacs and small blood vessels

The cells release growth factors, proteins that help cells survive and repair. In animal studies they help protect the air sacs, called alveoli, and the tiny blood vessels around them, where oxygen passes into the blood.

04

Exosomes, breathed in

Exosomes are tiny packages of signals released by the cells. As part of this protocol, some are delivered by nebulizer, a mist you breathe in, so they reach the airways directly, alongside the intravenous infusion.

What the evidence says

What the research shows so far

Human trials in chronic lung disease are growing. Several used mesenchymal stem cells from bone marrow rather than the umbilical cord, and each one is labeled below.

Lab & animal studiesFirst human studiesSeveral clinical trialsStandard of care

Pooling the COPD studies, people treated with stem cell-based therapies walked about 52 meters farther in six minutes, with a trend toward better lung function (FEV1).

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 studies with 371 COPD patients.

Stem Cell-Based Regenerative Therapy and Derived Products in COPD: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. · Cells, 2022 ↗

In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis that was worsening quickly, repeated intravenous mesenchymal stem cells were followed by better lung capacity and walking distance than placebo, with no serious side effects.

Randomized, placebo-controlled trial, 20 patients in Russia; donor bone-marrow mesenchymal stem cells given intravenously every three months.

First-in-human high-cumulative-dose stem cell therapy in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis with rapid lung function decline. · Stem Cells Translational Medicine, 2019 ↗

In hospitalized patients with severe COVID-19 lung injury, umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells given intravenously were safe and lowered inflammatory signals.

Double-blind, randomized phase 1/2a trial, 24 patients in intensive care in the United States. This was a sudden, acute lung injury; it is included because it used umbilical cord cells through a vein, as the Stem Cells Colombia protocol does.

Umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells for COVID-19 acute respiratory distress syndrome: A double-blind, phase 1/2a, randomized controlled trial. · Stem Cells Translational Medicine, 2021 ↗

In short: Across these trials, intravenous mesenchymal stem cells were well tolerated in people with lung disease, with encouraging signs in walking distance and lung capacity. Your doctor reviews your CT and breathing tests and explains what you can expect.

Questions

Your questions, answered.

Am I a candidate?

Adults with a chronic lung condition who are stable enough to travel may be candidates. Send your lung CT and your breathing tests; an internist or the functional-medicine physician reviews them and explains what you can expect, before you book.

What do stem cells do in the lungs?

Mesenchymal stem cells release signals that may help calm chronic inflammation and slow the signals that drive scarring. The aim is to support your lungs and help slow the progression.

What is the goal of the protocol?

To calm inflammation, support your body’s own repair and help slow the progression of COPD, emphysema or pulmonary fibrosis, alongside the care you already receive.

Why nebulized?

In the respiratory protocol, part of the exosomes is delivered by nebulizer, so they are breathed in and reach the airways directly, alongside the intravenous infusion.

When will I see results?

Improvement is usually expected between 3 and 6 months after treatment. Repeating your breathing tests and a walking test with your own doctor shows you the change in numbers.

How many visits will I need?

Two visits are usually recommended, 6 to 8 weeks apart, repeating the same four-day protocol. Returning patients receive a returning-patient discount on the second visit.

What studies do you need?

A chest CT scan, pulmonary function tests, alpha-1 antitrypsin (A1AT), CRP, a complete blood count and a metabolic panel. Cancer markers are done on day one in Medellín.

Do I stop my inhalers or oxygen?

No. Keep all your current treatment, including inhalers, medicines and oxygen if you use it. The doctor reviews it in your consultation, and any change is for you and your pulmonologist to decide.

Why do you check cancer markers first?

Every intravenous protocol starts with cancer markers, on day one, for your safety: the protocol is designed for people without active cancer. Your doctor also checks that you are ready to fly.

Is it legal, and how much does it cost?

Stem cell therapy is not approved by the FDA, but in Colombia it is legal.
