What the cells do

What stem cells may do in Crohn’s disease

In Crohn’s disease the immune system attacks the lining of the gut again and again. Mesenchymal stem cells send signals that are studied for calming that attack.

01

They talk to the immune system

Mesenchymal stem cells release signaling molecules, small chemical messages, that can slow down overactive immune cells called T cells. In Crohn’s, those cells keep the gut inflamed. Turning their activity down could mean fewer or milder flares.

02

They support the “peacekeeper” cells

In lab and early human studies, these cells encourage regulatory T cells, the part of the immune system whose job is to say “stop”. More of that brake may help the inflammation settle instead of feeding itself.

03

They lower inflammatory signals

The cells are studied for reducing inflammatory messengers such as TNF-alpha, the same target many Crohn’s medicines block. Less of these signals in the blood may mean a calmer gut and less of the whole-body tiredness that comes with it.

04

They support the healing environment

Through growth factors and tiny particles called exosomes, the cells may help create conditions in which damaged tissue can repair itself. This is the idea behind the fistula studies below, where the cells were placed directly into the fistula.

What the evidence says

What the research shows so far

Two kinds of research exist. Intravenous studies, like the Stem Cells Colombia protocol, for active Crohn’s in the gut. And a different approach: cells from fat injected straight into a perianal fistula. Both appear here, each clearly labeled.

Lab & animal studiesFirst human studiesSeveral clinical trialsStandard of care

People with Crohn’s who received umbilical cord stem cells through a vein had lower disease activity and needed less steroid a year later than those who did not.

Randomized controlled trial, 82 adults with Crohn’s on long-term steroids, in China; four weekly intravenous infusions of umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells. No serious side effects were reported.

Umbilical Cord Mesenchymal Stem Cell Treatment for Crohn's Disease: A Randomized Controlled Clinical Trial. · Gut and Liver, 2018 ↗

In people whose Crohn’s no longer responded to biologics, donor mesenchymal stem cells given intravenously were followed by lower symptom scores, and about half showed improvement on colonoscopy.

Phase 2 open study, 16 adults in Australia with active Crohn’s refractory to infliximab or adalimumab; four weekly intravenous infusions.

A phase 2 study of allogeneic mesenchymal stromal cells for luminal Crohn's disease refractory to biologic therapy. · Clinical Gastroenterology and Hepatology, 2013 ↗

Stem cells from donor fat, injected directly into complex perianal fistulas, closed more fistulas than placebo in a large European trial.

Phase 3 randomized, double-blind trial (ADMIRE-CD), 212 adults in Europe and Israel; one local injection of adipose-derived cells (darvadstrocel) into the fistula during surgery. This is a local, fat-derived treatment, different from the Stem Cells Colombia intravenous protocol with umbilical cord cells.

Expanded allogeneic adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (Cx601) for complex perianal fistulas in Crohn's disease: a phase 3 randomised, double-blind controlled trial. · The Lancet, 2016 ↗

In short: The intravenous studies in Crohn’s are encouraging, including a randomized trial with umbilical cord cells, and a large European trial showed fistula healing with a local injection of fat-derived cells. Questions

Your questions, answered.

Am I a candidate?

Adults with a confirmed diagnosis of Crohn’s disease may be candidates. Send your latest blood work on WhatsApp; the medical team reviews it, together with your current treatment, so your plan is designed for you.

What do stem cells do in Crohn’s disease?

Mesenchymal stem cells release signals that may help calm the overactive immune response that inflames the gut, and may support the gut’s own healing. The aim is to reduce the activity of Crohn’s disease and slow its progression.

Is this the same as the stem cell treatment for fistulas I read about?

They are different treatments. The fistula treatment in the large European trial used cells from donor fat, injected directly into the fistula during surgery. The Stem Cells Colombia protocol uses umbilical cord (Wharton’s jelly) cells given through a vein, to act on the whole immune system. If fistulas are your main concern, say so, and the doctor explains how the protocol fits your case.

When will I see results?

Improvement is usually expected between 3 and 6 months after treatment, and every body responds at its own pace. Repeating your CRP and fecal calprotectin with your own doctor shows you the change in numbers.

How many visits will I need?

Two visits are usually recommended, 6 to 8 weeks apart, repeating the same four-day protocol.

What studies do you need?

Six blood and stool tests: a CBC with differential, a comprehensive metabolic panel, CRP or ESR, serum albumin, fecal calprotectin and iron studies. Cancer markers are done on day one in Medellín.

Do I stop my current treatment?

Never on your own. Keep taking your Crohn’s medication; your doctor reviews it in your consultation, and any change is for you and your gastroenterologist to decide together.

Why do you check cancer markers first?

Every intravenous protocol starts with cancer markers, on day one, for your safety: the protocol is designed for people without active cancer.

Is it legal and safe?

Stem cell therapy is not approved by the FDA, but in Colombia it is legal. The infusion runs slowly, over three hours or more, with a physician present. Afterwards you rest at the hotel, one elevator away, with your coordinator checking on you.
