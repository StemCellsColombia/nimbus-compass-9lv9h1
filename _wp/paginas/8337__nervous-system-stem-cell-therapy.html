Neurological FAQ

Your questions, answered.

What can this do for my condition?

Research suggests that mesenchymal cells may help calm the brain's immune cells that drive inflammation and support the survival of the neurons you still have — protecting the function that remains, which is the real aim of this protocol. The earlier the diagnosis, the more there is to protect.

What is intrathecal delivery, and why does it matter?

The cells are placed directly into the cerebrospinal fluid that bathes your brain and spinal cord, in the operating room — far closer to where they need to work than an intravenous infusion can reach. Your neurologist reviews your case first, so your plan is designed for you.

Which route will I need?

That is decided by the neurologist after reviewing your diagnosis, your imaging and your labs — never by a price list, and never before your consultation.

Do I stop my current medication?

No. This is designed to work alongside the neurologist treating you at home, never instead. Dopaminergic medication, disease-modifying therapy and anticonvulsants are never modified or replaced by this — your own neurologist leads that.

Is it safe? What is checked before I travel?

Every protocol starts with cancer markers, for your safety. Your diagnosis, imaging, labs and current medication are reviewed before you book anything.

How many days do I need in Medellín?

The intravenous protocol runs across four days: screening and cancer markers, then premedication and a detox IV, then your stem cells, and finally your exosomes. The intrathecal protocol runs across five days: Day 1, cancer markers; Day 2, a detox IV and nebulized exosomes; Day 3, the intrathecal application of your cells in the operating room, with nebulized exosomes; Days 4 and 5, exosomes by IV and nebulized. The neurologist reviews and leads your case, and the application is performed by Dr. Reyes or Dr. Toro. For systemic conditions the doctors recommend two visits, the second 6–8 weeks after the first — and returning patients receive a returning-patient discount on the second visit. Hotel and treatment center are in the same building.

How much does it cost?

Neurological protocols in Medellín start from $4,000 USD intravenously: the stem cells by IV, cancer markers, premedication and a consultation with the doctor. Exosomes, a detox IV, the Marriott Hotel in the same building and airport transfers can be added to your plan, and your coordinator sends that price in writing. Intrathecal delivery starts from $7,300. Comparable treatment in the US typically runs $30,000–$40,000+.

When would we see changes?

Improvement builds gradually, usually between 3 and 6 months — every body responds at its own pace. In stroke, brain injury and cerebral palsy, this accompanies rehabilitation, which drives your progress.

Is this approved by the FDA?

Stem cell therapy is not approved by the FDA, but in Colombia it is legal, and that is where your treatment takes place, performed by specialist physicians.

My family member can't travel easily. Is it worth it?

Mention it in the first message. Airport pickup can be added to your plan, and the hotel and treatment center share one building precisely so the trip is short and manageable, and the whole plan is built around mobility. Your coordinator handles every detail, so the trip feels easy.

What can you expect?

Every body responds at its own pace; improvement usually builds over 3–6 months. What you can expect is the quality of the cells, a neurologist leading your case, and a medical team with you the whole way.
