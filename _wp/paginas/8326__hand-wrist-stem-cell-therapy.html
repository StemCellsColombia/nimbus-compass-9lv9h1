What the cells do

What stem cells may do inside a painful hand or wrist joint.

The cells are placed right in the joint or tendon. There, they act mostly by releasing signals, small proteins that talk to the tissue around them.

01

They may calm the inflammation in the joint lining

In arthritis, the thin lining of the joint (the synovium) gets inflamed and swollen. Mesenchymal stem cells release signals that may quiet the immune cells causing that inflammation. For you, this could mean less swelling, less morning stiffness and less pain when you grip.

02

They may slow the enzymes that wear down cartilage

Cartilage is the smooth layer that lets bones glide. In arthritis, the joint makes enzymes that break it down faster. In laboratory studies, stem cell signals reduce these enzymes and protect the cartilage cells, which may help the joint keep the cushion it still has.

03

They may support repair in tendons and ligaments

A partial tear heals slowly, and often with weak, disorganized tissue. Stem cell signals may help the local repair cells lay down more orderly collagen, the protein that makes a tendon strong. The goal is a wrist or finger that tolerates load again.

04

They may lower pain signals

Less inflammation usually means fewer pain signals from the joint. Many people notice first that daily tasks like writing, cooking or carrying bags become easier.

What the evidence says

What the research says about stem cells in small joints

The strongest human studies of stem cells for arthritis were done in the knee, and research in the hand is growing. Here is the best of it, and where each study comes from.

Lab & animal studiesFirst human studiesSeveral clinical trialsStandard of care

People with knee arthritis who received umbilical cord stem cells had less pain and better function at one year than those who received hyaluronic acid.

Randomized phase I/II trial in Chile, 26 adults with knee osteoarthritis. The cells came from the umbilical cord, the same kind of source the specialists use; two doses six months apart did best. It was done in the knee, not in the hand.

Umbilical Cord-Derived Mesenchymal Stromal Cells (MSCs) for Knee Osteoarthritis: Repeated MSC Dosing Is Superior to a Single MSC Dose and to Hyaluronic Acid in a Controlled Randomized Phase I/II Trial. · Stem Cells Translational Medicine, 2019 ↗

Compared with a placebo injection, stem cell injections may slightly improve pain and function in knee arthritis for up to six months.

Cochrane systematic review of 25 randomized trials (1,341 people) using stem cells from different sources. The authors found no clear signal of serious harm.

Stem cell injections for osteoarthritis of the knee. · Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2025 ↗

In arthritis at the base of the thumb, injecting the patient's own fat, which carries stromal cells, was followed by less pain and better hand function.

Systematic review of 10 studies, mostly without a control group. This is a different treatment from the Stem Cells Colombia protocol (the patient's own fat tissue, not cultured cord cells), shown here because it is the closest human evidence in the thumb.

Autologous Fat Grafting for Trapeziometacarpal Joint Osteoarthritis: A Systematic Review of Effectiveness and Outcomes. · The Journal of Hand Surgery Asian-Pacific Volume, 2026 ↗

In short: Cord stem cells have shown less pain and better function in human knees, and a related treatment eased pain and improved hand function in the thumb. A specialist reviews your X-ray or MRI for free and explains what you can expect for your hand or wrist.

Questions

Your questions, answered.

Am I a candidate?

If you have arthritis in the wrist, thumb or finger joints (grades 1 to 3), or a partial tear of a tendon or ligament in the hand or wrist, you may be. Grade 4 (“bone on bone”) can also be treated, after your specialist reviews your images. Send your X-ray or MRI and a specialist reviews it for free, so your plan is designed for you before you spend anything.

What do the stem cells actually do in my hand?

They release signals that may calm inflammation inside the joint, slow the enzymes that wear down cartilage, and support repair in partial tears of tendons and ligaments. The aim is less pain and better use of your hand.

When will I notice a difference?

Gradually. After the application you rest at the hotel, one elevator away, with your coordinator checking on you. Improvement is usually expected between 4 and 6 months, and some patients keep improving at 8 months.

How many visits and applications will I need?

One treatment day in Medellín, plus three online consultations: one before and two after. At about six months your team checks your progress: when pain and function improved by more than 20%, a second application may be recommended to build on it.

Which tests do I need?

An X-ray or an MRI of the hand or wrist, and the report if you have it. If you don't have one, your coordinator can arrange an MRI in Medellín from $200 per body area, or an X-ray from $50, with transfers included.

Should I stop my medication?

Do not stop any medication on your own. Your doctor reviews what you take in your online consultation. After the application you take only what your doctor recommends, and no anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs such as ibuprofen), so the cells can work on their own.

How do you keep me safe?

Your specialist reviews your health history before you book anything, so your plan is designed for you. The treatment is for people without active cancer, and anything else, such as an infection in the hand, is checked case by case.

Can I treat both hands on the same trip?

Yes. Both hands can be treated on the same day, starting from $5,500.

How much does it cost?

The wrist, the hand and the thumb all start from $3,790, all-inclusive. Your exact price is confirmed after your consultation.

Is it legal and safe?

Stem cell therapy is not approved by the FDA, but in Colombia it is legal. Your application is done by a specialist physician in a fully equipped operating room, with ultrasound guidance, after your case has been reviewed.
